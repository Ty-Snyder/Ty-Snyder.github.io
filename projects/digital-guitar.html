Digital Electric Guitar

Real-time DSP guitar effects, controlled over Wi-Fi

Project Overview

This project started as a class project for ME100. I took an electric guitar kit, removed the stock electronics, and replaced them with a Teensy 4.1 that processes the guitar signal digitally. Instead of a chain of physical pedals, any tone control or effect is just a function applied to the signal, and every function can be edited over Wi-Fi.

How it Works

The signal from the pickups (after the 5-position selector switch) goes into the Teensy, which runs a discrete Fourier transform to break the audio into its frequency components. Once the signal is just frequencies and their weights, altering it is easy: apply gains to frequency ranges for bass, middle, and treble control, or write functions for almost any pedal effect or unusual alteration, then convert it back to audio and output it. An ESP32 hosts a control web page over Wi-Fi, so you connect to its network and adjust everything from your phone.

Hardware Stack

  • Teensy 4.1
  • Teensy Audio Shield
  • Adafruit HUZZAH32 (ESP32)
  • 1S LiPo Battery
  • Electric Guitar Kit, 5-Position Switch

Sub Sections

Prototype

The prototype fits all of the electronics inside the guitar body: the Teensy 4.1 with its audio shield, an Adafruit HUZZAH32 (ESP32), and a 1S LiPo battery with a charging board, all wired under the pickguard. The Teensy handles the signal processing, while the ESP32 handles Wi-Fi and serves the control page for editing effects.

The Day Before the Presentation

The day before the final presentation, the audio shield shorted. With no time to get a replacement, I designed custom analog filters out of just capacitors and resistors and soldered them onto the Teensy, so the Teensy 4.1 could handle all of the input and output processing itself.

Version 2: External PCB

The biggest problem with the prototype was space. Fitting the electronics meant drilling out more room inside the guitar, which I can't do to just any guitar. Version 2 moves everything into an external device on a custom PCB, so it works with any electric guitar. You plug the guitar into it, and it can output to standard headphones or to an amp. The schematic is finished and the board layout is in progress.

  • Audio: SGTL5000 codec (the same chip as the Teensy audio shield) built onto the board, with its own regulator and ferrite-filtered analog supply.
  • Control: ESP32-C6 for the Wi-Fi control page, plus three rotary encoder knobs and a screen connector.
  • Power: Optional 1S LiPo with an MCP73871 charging circuit and a 3.3V regulator.
  • USB-C: One port with an onboard USB hub, so both the Teensy and the ESP32 can be programmed through the same connector.
